# Plain-English Readout

Produce the seven sections below after six turns, or eight after the single optional extension. Use full sentences and active, direct voice. Explain the significance before showing the internal label in parentheses. Never make the user look up a code to understand the next action.

## Writing rules

- Lead with the meaning, not the notation. Write "We have not established whether the agent can protect a customer's right to report fraud (legal permissibility check, G1)." Do not write "G1 is uncertain."
- Explain evidence in ordinary language. Write "We do not yet know whether issuers will pay enough to cover delivery costs (monetization assumption, P-06)." Do not open with confidence labels or rubric weights.
- Put optional claim IDs and evidence labels after the sentence they support, in parentheses. Keep citations next to factual claims. Spell out unfamiliar acronyms on first use.
- Keep weights, score arithmetic, and the full legend in the accompanying report unless the user asks to score the idea. Never equate disagreement, confidence, or a debate win with empirical validation.
- Use no em dashes, colons in prose, or semicolons. Preserve URLs and required machine syntax. Use natural headings and sentences rather than standalone bold labels.
- Distinguish something absent from the brief from something proven not to exist. Write "You have not identified an issuer partner," not "No issuer partner exists."

## Quick recap

In two or three sentences, restate the idea and explain what you did. Say that the Proponent argued for customer value and business upside while the Devil's Advocate tested economics, adoption, execution, and risks. State the actual count of six or eight turns and disclose simulated perspectives. If extended, briefly explain which consequential issue needed the extra pair. Mention material assumptions without treating them as supplied facts.

Name the user's core hypothesis and note how supplied principles and evidence shaped the debate. When no jurisdiction was supplied, say "We assume a US launch unless you specify otherwise." Do not describe this default as a user-supplied fact or proof of regulatory permission.

## Debate synthesis

Give the strongest surviving argument for and against in full sentences. Explain what each side conceded, what remains unresolved, and which side made the stronger supported argument under the declared acceptance criteria.

State whether the Proponent prevailed, the Devil's Advocate prevailed, a narrower revised idea prevailed, or neither side prevailed. Explain why. A narrower idea may defeat the original scope without defeating the customer problem. Do not force a winner when evidence cannot distinguish the cases.

This is an assessment of the arguments, not a final investment verdict or validated demand. The user still owns the scorecard and the decision. Introduce no new evidence after the last executed turn. Name meaningful taste conflicts here rather than adding an empty taste section.

## Success metrics, guardrails, and tradeoffs

Use the following subsections inside this section, without adding duplicate top-level sections.

### Definition of success

State in plain English what outcome, for whom, compared with what, within what window, and under what safety and evidence conditions justifies advancing. Tie it to the core hypotheses. Distinguish success at prototype, pilot, and production stages when applicable. This is the decision standard, not just a list of metrics.

### Success metrics and guardrails

Define one north-star customer-outcome metric. Explain its numerator, denominator, data source, observation window, and baseline or lack of one. Include a business-value metric, meaningful usage or adoption, and data sufficiency where required to establish the result. Define data volume in useful units such as outcome-labeled episodes or coverage of relevant segments, not bytes collected. Specify quality, representativeness, and maturity. If usage or data volume is not relevant, explain why.

| Metric or guardrail | What it measures and how | Target and rationale | Action if missed or breached |
|---|---|---|---|
| <Customer outcome, business value, or safety limit> | <Definition, source, and window> | <Measured baseline or provisional target> | <Action> |

Show material guardrails and stop conditions in the readout, not just the report. Explain the tradeoffs the user must consciously choose, such as speed versus accuracy or savings versus customer protection. Measure outcomes rather than engagement alone. Do not optimize for fewer complaints or fewer disputes when that could suppress legitimate reporting.

### Tradeoffs

Name the choices the user must make and how they relate to their guiding principles. Keep the definition of success, measurements, and tradeoffs consistent. Label proposed targets as provisional and do not imply that collecting more data is success by itself.

## How to validate and move forward

### High-level plan

Recommend a sequence that addresses the conditions in "What must be true before production" and the definition of success, beginning with the cheapest potentially disqualifying uncertainty. Link each stage to a specific requirement, resolving evidence, needed product or operational work, and advancement criterion. Cover a bounded pilot and launch readiness when applicable rather than stopping at research.

### First action and decision rule

Name the specific first action, its dependency, the evidence it produces, and how the result changes the plan.

Explain when to advance, narrow the idea, gather more evidence, or stop. Link each step to the surviving hypothesis and the corresponding production requirement. Do not invent owners, dates, costs, sample sizes, or numerical thresholds. A conditional recommendation does not replace the user's final verdict.

## Transcript and report access

Retain the actual six- or eight-turn scripted outputs, questions, answers, concessions, claim IDs, and citations in a Markdown report. Include the charter, stopping decision, complete objection ledger, detailed validation plan, full rubric, and blank scorecard. Share the report with the readout.

Offer "Show full debate" to display the retained transcript and "Return to summary" to return to these seven sections. Do not regenerate the debate. These commands affect subsequent responses and do not erase previous messages. Use a native expand or collapse control only when the surface demonstrably supports it. Otherwise provide the report and commands without promising a clickable control.
